Using Bubbleshift

Convert a file

Click the center of the drop area to choose files, or drag files onto it. Choose an output format for each queued file, select the destination, and press Convert. Completed files can be revealed in Finder with the folder button. Originals are kept; existing output names get a numeric suffix.

In the queue, the stop button cancels a conversion: a waiting one is dropped at once, and one already running finishes in the background with its file discarded. Failed or cancelled rows have a retry button, the folder button shows a finished file in Finder, and Clear finished removes everything that is done.

Shift-drag and Command-drag

Keep Bubbleshift running. Start dragging files in Finder, then tap Shift to see formats, Command to see actions or Option for file commands, and drop on a bubble. Press the key after the drag has started; Command held while clicking changes the Finder selection instead. You can switch between Shift and Command while still dragging. Your files are never moved by the drag. Only options shared by all dragged files appear. Enable or disable this in Settings. In the App Store edition, choose an output folder when prompted the first time; later conversions can reuse that permission.

While a dragged file converts, a small glass bubble stays beside where you dropped it, with a ring that fills as the work progresses (or spins when the progress can't be measured). A ✓ means the new file is ready; click the bubble to show it in Finder. If something didn't work, for example a file that is already well compressed, the bubble turns amber and opens into a short message. In the window, each queued file's icon gets the same ring. For long conversions, hover over the bubble and click In background (or just wait: after ten seconds it moves there on its own). The bubble flies up to the menu bar, where the Bubbleshift icon becomes a progress ring and its menu lists what is converting; a notification tells you when the file is ready, and clicking the notification shows it in Finder. Bubbleshift asks once for permission to show notifications. If notifications are turned off (or a Focus holds them back), the finished bubble appears for a moment just below the menu bar instead.

Different keys: in Settings › Background › Keys, choose which key opens each ring: Shift, Control, Option, Command or Fn/🌐. Choosing a key another ring uses swaps the two, and Reset brings back Shift, Command and Option. Your choice is saved with your other settings.

File commands

Tap Option while dragging for file commands. They act on the dragged files right away: AirDrop sends them, Path copies their full paths, Copy puts the files on the clipboard to paste in any Finder window, Copy to and Move ask for a folder (Move is only in the direct-download edition), and Terminal opens Terminal in that folder.

Actions

Actions create a new file and never change the original.

Compress: makes photos, PDFs, videos and audio smaller. While dragging, rest on Compress for a moment to choose Low, Medium or High compression, each showing how much smaller the file gets and the size it comes to, for example "−43%, 111 KB". Low keeps the most quality, High saves the most space; levels that would come out the same, or barely smaller, aren't shown. Dropping straight on Compress uses the level you chose last (Medium at first). Photos are re-saved as JPEG or HEIC at a lower quality (PNG, TIFF and BMP without transparency become JPEG), PDFs keep their text and re-encode embedded images at a lower resolution, videos become HEVC (up to 4K, 1080p or 720p), and audio becomes AAC M4A. If the result would not be meaningfully smaller, nothing is saved and Bubbleshift tells you so.

Merge (two or more files): combines PDFs and images into one PDF, videos into one video, or audio files into one track, in the order you set in the panel that opens. Audio files of one lossless format (WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC) stay in that format; other audio is joined as M4A. Joined clips play one after another, upright and fitted to the first clip's frame (with black bars rather than stretching), with their sound.

Split: saves every page of a PDF as its own PDF, in a new folder. For one video, it opens a preview with a filmstrip: drag the playhead to where you want to cut and click Split Here; the two parts are saved beside the video as "part 1" and "part 2", cut exactly at that frame.

Resize: scales images, keeping their format and metadata. While dragging, rest on Resize for a moment to choose a size: 50%, or a longest side of 1080, 2048 or 3840 pixels. For one image, the last bubble, Custom, opens a preview where you drag the image's corner to any size; it keeps the proportions, and the corner sticks to common sizes such as 1920 or 1280 pixels. Return to the center to go back to the actions. Dropping straight on Resize uses the size you chose last (2048 pixels at first). In the queue, pick a size bubble, or the … bubble to enter an exact width and height; the lock keeps the original proportions. For videos, Resize offers video sizes instead: 720p, 1080p or 4K (by the short side, so portrait clips stay portrait); a video is never enlarged.

Rotate (images and videos): turns 90° right, or the way you chose last. While dragging, rest on Rotate for a moment to choose Right, Left, 180°, Mirror (left to right) or Flip (top to bottom). Photos are turned pixel by pixel, so they look right everywhere; videos are turned instantly and without any loss in quality, sound included.

Crop (one image or video at a time): opens a preview where you drag the frame's corners or edges to choose the area, or drag inside it to move it. Pick Free, Original, 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9 or 9:16 (vertical video) to keep a shape. A cropped image keeps the format and metadata of the original; a cropped video keeps its sound and plays upright. In the queue, use "Choose area…" on a Crop row.

Trim (one video or audio file at a time): opens a preview that loops the part you keep. Drag the handles on the filmstrip to set the start and end, drag between them to move the whole range, and click the strip to move the playhead; press Space to play or pause. The cut is frame-accurate. MOV videos stay MOV; other formats are saved as MP4. For audio, a waveform takes the place of the filmstrip; WAV, AIFF, CAF and FLAC keep their format, other audio is saved as M4A.

Speed (videos): plays the whole video faster or slower; the sound keeps its pitch. While dragging, rest on Speed for a moment to choose 0.5×, 1.5×, 2× or 4×. Dropping straight on Speed uses the speed you chose last (2× at first). The result keeps the original frame rate, up to 60 frames per second.

Frame (one video at a time): opens a preview with a filmstrip. Drag the playhead (or play and pause) to the moment you want, then click Save Frame; the frame is saved as a full-size PNG beside the video, the right way up.

Louder (audio): raises the volume so the loudest moment sits just below distortion (−1 dBFS). If the audio is already that loud, nothing is saved. WAV, AIFF, CAF and FLAC keep their format; other audio is saved as M4A.

Mono and Stereo (audio): a stereo file offers Mono, which mixes both sides into one channel (handy for voice recordings and podcasts); a mono file offers Stereo, which plays it on both sides. The length and, for WAV, AIFF, CAF and FLAC, the format are kept; other audio is saved as M4A.

Fade (audio): fades in over the first second and out over the last (a tenth of the length each for short clips), so music and recordings start and end softly.

Clean: removes location, camera details and other metadata from images (keeping orientation) and removes location and personal metadata from videos.

Text: extracts text into a TXT file. Text already in a PDF is used directly; images and scanned pages are read with on-device text recognition.

Searchable (PDFs and images): makes a PDF that looks exactly like the scan or photo, with the recognized text laid invisibly over it, so you can search it, select it and copy it. The text is read on your Mac. Pages that already have real text are left as they are; a PDF that is already searchable is declined.

Mute: removes the sound from a video without re-encoding the picture where possible.

Cutout (macOS 14 or later): removes the background behind the main subject of an image and saves a transparent PNG.

Supported conversions

Images: PNG, JPEG, HEIC, AVIF, TIFF, BMP, GIF, WebP, JPEG XL, PSD, ICO and camera RAW files (Canon, Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm, Olympus, Panasonic, Pentax, Samsung and DNG) convert to PNG, JPEG, HEIC, AVIF, TIFF, GIF, BMP, PDF or ICO. Photos keep the orientation they are shown in. Icons are at most 256 × 256 pixels and squared with a transparent margin. Animated inputs use the first frame. WebP cannot be written.

Audio: MP3, M4A, AAC, WAV, AIFF, FLAC and CAF convert to M4A (AAC), WAV, AIFF, FLAC or ALAC (lossless, in .m4a). macOS includes no MP3 encoder, so MP3 is input only.

Documents: DOCX, RTF, TXT, ODT, HTML and Markdown convert locally to PDF, DOCX, ODT, RTF, TXT or HTML. Markdown headings, lists, emphasis, code and links keep their look. PDF text can export to TXT or DOCX, and PDF pages can export to PNG or JPEG. For scanned PDFs, use the Text action for on-device text recognition. Complex Word layouts may change. PDF-to-DOCX preserves available text, not a full reconstruction of the original page design.

Spreadsheets: CSV, TSV and XLSX convert directly to XLSX, CSV, TSV, or PDF as offered. Multiple sheets export to a folder of CSV or TSV files. These exports use cell values; formulas use their cached results. Formatting, charts, images, and live formulas are not preserved.

Subtitles: SRT and WebVTT convert to each other, or to plain text (TXT): a clean transcript with only the spoken lines, without timings, styling or sound effects.

Apple documents: Pages, Numbers, and Keynote files require the corresponding installed Apple app and Automation permission. PowerPoint input currently uses Keynote. Output choices vary by input.

Video: MOV, MP4, M4V, AVI, MPG, MTS, M2TS, TS and 3GP inputs can export to supported MOV/MP4, HEVC-in-MP4, GIF, or M4A audio when macOS supports the codec inside. MKV, WebM and WMV are not supported. GIF exports are limited to one-minute inputs, 12 fps, and 640 × 640 maximum bounds. Silent videos cannot export audio.

Folders and archives: Shift-drag a folder and drop it on ZIP to make a .zip archive beside it, the same kind Finder's Compress makes. Drag two or more items, files or folders, and ZIP puts them all in one Archive.zip beside the first, leaving out Mac-only metadata so it opens cleanly on any system. Drop a .zip archive on Unzip, or a TAR, TAR.GZ, TGZ, TAR.BZ2, TAR.XZ, 7Z or RAR archive on Extract, to unpack it into a folder beside it; an archive that holds a single folder unpacks to that folder. A lone .gz or .bz2 file decompresses to the file inside. Password-protected archives are not supported.

Troubleshooting

If a folder is unavailable, choose another output folder or reconnect its drive. If a remembered folder permission stops working, forget saved folder permissions in Settings and select it again. If Apple-app export fails, open the required app once and check Automation permissions. Password-protected PDFs must be unlocked before conversion.

Closing the window keeps Bubbleshift in the menu bar. Use Quit Bubbleshift to stop it. For further assistance, email us and include your macOS version, Bubbleshift version, input/output formats, and the error message. Do not include sensitive files by default.

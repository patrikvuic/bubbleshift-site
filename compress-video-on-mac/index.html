How to compress a video on a Mac

iPhone videos and screen recordings quickly grow to hundreds of megabytes, too big for email, chat apps or upload forms. Here is how to make them much smaller on your Mac while keeping them sharp.

With Bubbleshift

  1. Start dragging the video in Finder.
  2. Tap ⌘ Command while dragging and drop on Compress.
  3. A smaller copy appears beside the original, re-encoded as efficient HEVC. If it wouldn't be meaningfully smaller, Bubbleshift tells you instead of saving a copy.

With what macOS already has

Open the video in QuickTime Player and choose File → Export As, then a smaller resolution such as 1080p or 720p. It works, but you choose sizes blindly and it can't keep the original resolution at a lower bitrate.

Why use Bubbleshift for this

One drag, no export dialogs, and the result keeps the sound and the picture's orientation. Videos never leave your Mac, which matters for private recordings.

Questions

Will the compressed video still play on Windows and Android?

Yes, on current devices. HEVC is widely supported; if you need maximum compatibility, convert to MP4 afterwards with ⇧ Shift.

Does compressing change my original video?

No. Bubbleshift writes a new file beside it.

Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. Compression runs entirely on your Mac.
